Functional rescue: receptor turnover and effective-deviation scaling.

Two mechanisms prevent permanent receptor burnout traps:

  1. Effective-deviation scaling: receptor downregulation is driven by receptor activation (effective signaling), not by raw ligand concentration. The deviation used for downregulation is scaled by receptor health: effective_deviation = raw_deviation × sensitivity × desensitization × internalization. When receptors are burnt out, effective deviation is small, so downregulation is weak. This is biologically correct — receptor downregulation is a response to overstimulation, and burnt-out receptors aren’t being overstimulated.

  2. Receptor turnover: a slow background recovery toward 1.0 that models the continuous receptor replacement/recycling that happens in biology (receptor half-life is days to weeks). This is always active but normally overwhelmed by deviation-gated downregulation. When downregulation is weak (low effective deviation due to burnout), turnover slowly restores receptor function.

    Each factor’s turnover is proportional to its adaptation rate, ordered by the biological cascade (5.0×, 1.0×, 0.2× for desensitization, internalization, sensitivity respectively — dephosphorylation is fast, membrane recycling intermediate, receptor synthesis slow). This ensures that:

    • In normal overstimulation: desensitization carries the acute adaptation on the measured minutes timescale, and recovers quickly once the drive drops.
    • In extreme burnout: health-scaled downregulation weakens as effective deviation collapses, letting turnover restore function; under sustained strong drive the factors correctly stay downregulated (chronic agonist → chronic tolerance).

These mechanisms are always active — no thresholds, no recovery zones, no limit cycles. The system smoothly transitions between normal adaptation and recovery as receptor health and deviation change.
