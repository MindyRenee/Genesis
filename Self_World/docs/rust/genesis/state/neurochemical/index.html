Neurochemical system — Genesis’s affective engine.

§Modeling note

This module is an affective control substrate — a compact 18-dimensional state space for control — not a concentration or pharmacokinetic model. The scalar-per-chemical abstraction is a computational analogue of neuromodulation, not a literal model of brain chemistry. It ignores compartments (synaptic cleft vs extrasynaptic vs plasma), receptor subtypes (except for the D1/D2 weighting applied to dopamine), cell-type specificity, and regional anatomy (a single “serotonin” scalar elides the dorsal vs median raphe projection differences). The coupling matrix and Hill-function gating are control-theoretic abstractions inspired by pharmacological dose-response curves, not a simulation of receptor-level kinetics. The “pharmacological reality” language used below refers to structural inspiration, not quantitative pharmacological accuracy.

§What makes this novel

Most computational affect models use independent dimensions (valence/arousal) or simple rule-based emotion systems. This module implements something structurally different:

§Coupled dynamics

The 18 neurochemicals are not independent. A 18×18 coupling matrix defines how each chemical modulates the others’ rates of change. Dopamine inhibits serotonin. Cortisol suppresses BDNF. GABA inhibits glutamate. Endorphins disinhibit dopamine. Orexin excites histamine. The system has emergent behaviour that cannot be predicted from any single chemical in isolation.

§Receptor adaptation

Each chemical has a receptor_sensitivity field. The effective level — what consumers actually see — is level × receptor_sensitivity, not the raw concentration. Sustained high dopamine causes downregulation (tolerance). Sustained low serotonin causes upregulation (sensitization). This mirrors how real pharmacology works. Receptor-level dynamics are well established in computational psychiatry and biophysical modelling; we are not aware of another AI control architecture that integrates receptor adaptation into a closed-loop neuromodulatory substrate coupled to physical hardware in this form, but the individual mechanisms are not novel to neuroscience.

§Metaplasticity

The coupling matrix itself is plastic. It lives in the mmap’d struct and can be modified by the dynamics engine over time. The brain doesn’t just learn by changing weights — it learns by changing HOW it learns.

§BDNF-gated plasticity

The system’s ability to form new memories is gated by BDNF, which is suppressed by chronic cortisol. This captures, structurally, why chronic stress impairs learning — and why recovery (rising BDNF via serotonin, exercise analogues) restores it.

§Emergent phase transitions

Mental states (flow, stress, drowsy, sleeping) emerge from the coupled dynamics rather than being manually switched. You don’t set “flow mode” — flow emerges when dopamine, ACh, and NE are high while cortisol is low.

Functions§

basal_cortisol 🔒
Basal glucocorticoid floor for receptor-occupancy math. A living system never sees literal zero — the substrate’s 0 means a quiescent HPA axis, physiologically equivalent to the circadian trough (~CORTISOL_RESTING_LEVEL). Occupancy below the floor is computed as resting, not adrenalectomy — the substrate has no adrenalectomy state, and the envelope’s left edge is the trough.
circulating_hormone_clearance 🔒
Measured plasma clearance for the circulating endocrine chemicals, or None for everything else (neural transmitters keep the generic spring; adenosine, cortisol, CRH, melatonin, and the network-driven monoamines have their own mechanisms).
corticosteroid_ltp_facilitation 🔒
Corticosteroid support of hippocampal LTP — an inverted-U against absolute cortisol (Diamond et al., 1992: primed-burst potentiation peaked at intermediate corticosterone and fell on both sides). De Kloet et al. (1999) recast the curve as the MR/GR occupancy ratio: plasticity is best when MRs are saturated and GRs only partly occupied, and degrades as GR-dominated stress levels recruit the low-affinity receptors. Resting cortisol sits at the optimum; supra-peak levels suppress asymptotically — graded, never a hard cutoff.
cortisol_stress_fraction 🔒
Cortisol’s effect strength as a fraction of its physiological range: 0 at the resting floor (basal glucocorticoid tone is not aversive and does not suppress plasticity), 1 at the reachable peak. Basal levels are required for normal function — the aversive and anti-plastic effects belong to elevated cortisol, so the excess above rest is what counts.
glucocorticoid_consolidation_boost 🔒
Glucocorticoid enhancement of consolidation — the Roozendaal inverted-U dose response (2000): post-training glucocorticoids at moderate stress doses enhance consolidation, while low doses contribute nothing and supra-peak doses lose the effect. Shaped as x·e^{1−x} over the envelope fraction so it rises from ≈0 at baseline, peaks ≈1 just under the physiological ceiling (s ≈ 0.8–1.0), and decays toward 0 off the envelope.
gr_occupancy 🔒
Glucocorticoid-receptor occupancy at an absolute cortisol level. Measured occupancy is ~10% at the circadian trough and ~67–74% at the stress/circadian peak (Reul & de Kloet, 1985) — too steep a transition for single-site binding, so this uses a Hill coefficient of 2 (GRs signal as dimers): c²/(c² + 0.06²) gives ≈0.10 at rest and ≈0.64 at the 0.08 peak.
mr_occupancy 🔒
Mineralocorticoid-receptor occupancy at an absolute cortisol level. MRs bind glucocorticoids with ~10× higher affinity than GRs and are already >90% occupied at circadian baseline (Reul & de Kloet, 1985), so Kd sits an order of magnitude below the resting level of ~0.02.
neuro_noise_hash 🔒
Simple deterministic hash for the stochastic noise process.
