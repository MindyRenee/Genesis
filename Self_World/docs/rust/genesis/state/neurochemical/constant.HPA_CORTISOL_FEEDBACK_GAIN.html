Cortisol negative feedback gain on ACTH (long-loop HPA feedback).

High cortisol suppresses ACTH release from the anterior pituitary, which in turn reduces cortisol synthesis. This is the primary negative feedback loop of the HPA axis — in vivo it sets on ~4 min after the cortisol rise begins and is rate-sensitive (Posner et al., Horm Res Paediatr 2024), abstracted here as an instantaneous level drain.

The gain is dimensionless and applied against ACTH_APPROACH_RATE: the drain is cort_eff × gain × rate × dt, so the ACTH equilibrium is acth* = target − gain·cort_eff regardless of how fast the pituitary approach runs. Writing the drain as an absolute per-tick rate instead made it 12× stronger than the approach once the approach was slowed to the measured ~60 s onset scale, and a modest cortisol elevation crushed ACTH to zero.

(Herman et al., 2016 — HPA axis negative feedback; de Kloet et al., 1998 — corticosteroid receptor-mediated feedback)
