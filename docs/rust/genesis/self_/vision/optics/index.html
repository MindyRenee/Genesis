Ocular optics — the eye’s front end before transduction.

Three transmission modulators, each its own dynamics:

  • The lids (Eyelid): a voluntary orbicularis oculi held at a commanded aperture, plus the blink — a brief palpebral closure the brainstem schedules. The blink is a Gaussian dip in transmission (~120 ms to close, ~240 ms total), not a step: real closure sweeps the cornea and the retinal illuminance follows the lid’s area, not a switch.
  • The pupil (Pupil): the pretectal light reflex — log- compressive constriction on retinal illuminance, so the aperture tracks the log of the light, not the light. Steady state solves sphincter = dilator + k·log(L); integration is first-order toward it at the iris muscle’s own rate.
  • Accommodation folds in as defocus: the lens state reports a blur radius so whatever samples the field can convolve at the PSF the ciliary body actually set.
