Expand description

Total surviving trace mass for an episode — the sum of each presentation’s power-law-decayed strength.

For n presentations over the episode’s lifetime, exact mass is Σ_k a_k^(−d), but only the count and the two endpoint ages are persisted. Two estimators bound the unknown interior:

  • Blend (Anderson & Schooler 1991’s merged-trace approximation): presentations treated as uniformly spaced, so M ≈ 2n / (√a₀ + √a_L). Best when reactivations are spread evenly through the episode’s life.
  • Endpoint bound: the newest trace contributes its exact a_L^(−d); every earlier trace is at least as old as a₀ and therefore contributes at least a₀^(−d), giving M ≥ a_L^(−d) + (n−1)·a₀^(−d). Exact for n ≤ 2, and the honest floor for a memory rehearsed once after a long silence — where the blend would spread the fresh trace’s strength back across the whole span and under-count it.

The mass is the larger of the two — the estimator that least underestimates for the history the episode actually lived.

age_first_days/age_last_days are ages of the oldest and newest trace in days; ages are floored so a just-encoded trace never produces an infinite mass.
