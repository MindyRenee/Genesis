Suprathreshold recruitment gain for coupling drive.

The coupling force from chemical j is driven by j’s deviation from baseline. Linear-in-deviation coupling under-measures suprathreshold events: a strong release (impulse, stress surge, arousal burst) recruits extrasynaptic and volume-transmission receptors only once the synaptic envelope overflows, so the downstream influence of an elevated source is superlinear in its deviation (Hill-type cooperativity; Fuxe et al., 2010 on volume transmission).

Implementation: positive deviations are driven as dev·(1 + GAIN·dev) — at the resting wander (~±0.03) the multiplier is ~1.06, so near-baseline dynamics, the resting linearized spectrum, and the depletion death-spiral protections are essentially untouched; at an assay-scale deviation (0.30) it is ~1.6. Negative (depletion) deviations stay linear — they are already bounded by the -AUTORECEPTOR_THRESHOLD cap, and suppressed signaling does not super-recruit.

Calibration: sized so a suprathreshold probe moves a weakly coupled responder across the assay’s 0.02 measurement floor (the B1 effective-connectivity measurement in notebooks/consciousness_correlates.ipynb). The causal assay showed linear drive produced mean 0.61 significant responders; with the diffuse tier at 0.10 and this gain, first-hop propagation roughly doubles.
