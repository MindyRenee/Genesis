The Metatron lattice — the discrete skeleton inside the latent bulk.

The κ-stereographic cap is the bulk: a 6-ball whose boundary — the equator rim at ‖x‖ = 1/√κ — is a 5-sphere, the holographic screen every interior point projects onto along its radial ray. Inside the bulk sits the lattice’s three figures, all sharing the same center — the origin, the superposition point:

  • The merkaba — the two interpenetrating demicubes of the 6-cube. In 3D the stella octangula is the cube’s vertices split by sign parity into two tetrahedra; the same construction in 6D splits the 64 vertices {±a}⁶ by parity into two 32-vertex demicubes, tips touching the rim. The upper (even parity) and lower (odd parity) halves are the field’s two chiralities.

  • The vesica — their intersection, the cell where both hold: {x : Σ|xᵢ| ≤ a(d−2), |xᵢ| ≤ a}. For d = 3 this recovers the octahedron at the heart of the stella octangula; in 6D it is the superposition region around the center.

  • The two circles — the vesica’s own construction: circles of radius R in the leading plane (e₀, e₁), centers at ±(R/2)·e₀ so each center rests on the other’s rim. They are the two counter-rotating latent halves made visible; their overlap is the lens — R wide between the centers, √3·R between its tips on e₁.

  • The frame — the vertical rectangle bounding the lens, R wide by √3·R tall, the vesica’s own √3 proportion. Its four corners {±(R/2)e₀ ± (√3R/2)e₁} rest on the rim at ±60° and ±120° — the picture boundary the vesica hangs inside.

  • The horizon — the horizontal line through the superposition: the segment joining the two circle centers, −(R/2)e₀ to +(R/2)e₀. Its endpoints are the points each circle is drawn around — and also the lens’s horizontal extremes and the frame’s side midpoints; its midpoint is the source itself.

  • The twelve rays — ±eᵢ·R, the cross-polytope vertices on the rim: the directions the center radiates toward, one antipodal pair per coordinate axis. Motion stays continuous — the rays are a scaffold, not a cage.

The superposition — the center itself — is the inner world. Every ray radiates outward from it: each ±eᵢ pair is two directions from the source, not one line through it. The center has no image on the screen; the source is everywhere on it at once.

The spin is the isoclinic bivector J = ω·(J₀₁ + J₂₃ + J₄₅): every mutually orthogonal plane turns at the same rate, and the two demicubes take opposite signs — the counter-rotation is the two helicities of the field.
